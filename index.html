What the model sees: centre square, grayscale, 64×64. If this doesn't look like an eye, move closer.

Fatigue uses DINOv2 (a large pretrained vision model) on the colour image, with a classifier trained on 120 labelled phone photos plus 2,632 reviewed web photos. Alert vs fatigued scored 72.5% (vs 53% guessing) on days it never saw. It's a weak signal, not a diagnosis. One scan takes a few seconds in the browser; live mode averages the last 10 scans.

Blink & PERCLOS meter

Checks your eye about 15–20 times a second with the open/closed model (97.7%) and measures how your eyes close over the last 60 seconds. PERCLOS, the share of time the eye is closed, is the best-proven eye signal of drowsiness in sleep research.

Higher PERCLOS, longer blinks and more long closures all point toward drowsiness. Everyone blinks differently, so compare against your own rested baseline: run a 60-second test when you're well rested and save it. Hold still, keep one eye in the box, and look at the screen. The model calls the eye closed below 30% open and open again above 70%.
